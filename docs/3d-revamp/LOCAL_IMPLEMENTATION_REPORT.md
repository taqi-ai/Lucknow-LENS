# 3D city realism upgrade: local implementation report

Branch `feat/3d-city-realism`, from baseline commit `76290107` (recorded in `data/backup/BASELINE_COMMIT.txt`). Target: the existing app on localhost. Nothing was deployed, and hosting and deployment configuration are unchanged.

Companion documents:

- [`DATA_SOURCES.md`](DATA_SOURCES.md): sources, licences, attribution
- [`VISUAL_QA.md`](VISUAL_QA.md): before/after screenshots, cameras, performance, remaining defects
- [`HEIGHT_QUALITY_REPORT.md`](HEIGHT_QUALITY_REPORT.md): building-height provenance, generated by the pipeline

**Deliberate deviations from the brief, stated up front:**

- **Terrain stays flat at Y = 0.** Copernicus GLO-30 is a surface model including buildings and canopy, and Lucknow's relief is about 25 m over 40 km. The DEM is used only for baked ground shading. Section 7 gives the reasoning.
- **No bloom or SSAO pass.** Night glow uses emissive surfaces, road light pools and a district glow, to keep integrated GPUs viable.
- **No Poly Haven textures.** All surfaces are procedural, so there is nothing to download or license.

## 1. What changed, in one paragraph

Every building now has its real Overture ID, all of its polygon parts and holes, and a height from an identified source:

- 61% are observed in Google's Open Buildings 2.5D model raster.
- 39% are inferred from neighbouring observed heights.

The uniform `12 + hash % 15` m band is gone. Building parts replace their parents. A deterministic Lucknow typology (8 classes) drives several things:

- storey heights and window rhythm
- bazaar shopfronts with signboards and tin awnings
- chhajjas and balconies
- roof furniture: parapets, stair cabins, black water tanks, barsati rooms, solar arrays, tin shelters and AC units
- metal-shed roofs for industry

A world-space district map keeps colours coherent across neighbourhoods and drives night-light density. A continuous ground surface replaces the dark disc. It is built from ESA WorldCover, Overture land use and water, and Copernicus DEM shading.

Roads have asphalt, markings only where Indian roads carry them, dusty shoulders, varied widths and night light pools. Trees come in six North-Indian species and follow real tree cover, and none stand on roofs or in roads. Seven landmarks have procedural models.

Lighting uses the real solar position for Lucknow, restored haze, shadows by tier, and a new night model. Quality tiers (low, medium, high) scale the expensive parts.

## 3. Real, derived and procedural

| Aspect | Status |
|---|---|
| Footprints, holes, multi-part buildings, building parts | **Real** (Overture 2026-10-21.0) |
| Building IDs | **Real** (Overture GERS IDs; multi-polygons get `#k` suffixes) |
| Height, 579,482 buildings (`gob25d`) | **Observed by a model**: Google 2.5D raster (2023), not a survey |
| Height, 3 + 14 + 106 buildings | **Observed** (`overture_height`), **derived** from parts / floors × typology floor height |
| Height, about 375k buildings (`model`) | **Inferred**: median of observed heights in the surrounding 375 m, footprint-size factor, deterministic jitter; typology prior where neighbours are missing |
| Typology | **Derived** from source class, land use, neighbourhood statistics, plus manually drawn zones (old city, bazaars, heritage precincts) |
| Roof furniture, facades, balconies, shopfronts | **Procedural**, seeded per building ID (spatially coherent probabilities by typology and size) |
| Ground surface | **Real classification** (WorldCover, Overture land use and water) with **procedural** near detail |
| Terrain | **Flat datum (Y = 0)**. DEM used for shading only (section 7) |
| Road geometry, class, level | **Real**. Widths **estimated** per class and type (Overture has no widths) |
| Markings, asphalt, light pools | **Procedural** |
| Tree positions | **Derived** (483,520 trees): WorldCover canopy, parks, verges, yards, masked against every road, water and footprint. Species are **procedural** |
| Landmarks | **Procedural reconstructions** anchored to real footprints. **Not photogrammetry** |
| Window lighting at night | **Procedural**, density from the district map (derived from building data) |

## 5. Performance

All figures are measured by `scripts/visual_harness.mjs` (headless Chrome, 1600×900, ANGLE D3D11) on an **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Laptop GPU**, with the dev server running. Quality tier is high unless stated. The baseline used the same camera positions. See `VISUAL_QA.md` for the full table.

| View | Baseline p95 ms / draws / triangles (M) | After p95 ms / draws / triangles (M) |
|---|---|---|
| City overview, day | 9.9 / 90 / 2.69 | 9.8 / 92 / 2.75 |
| Old city, 900 m, day | 10.0 / 419 / 3.75 | 9.8 / 481 / 4.46 |
| Street, 170 m, day | 10.0 / 597 / 4.16 | 9.8 / 608 / 4.80 |
| Neighbourhood, 3 km, day | 9.9 / 494 / 4.63 | 9.8 / 422 / 3.98 |
| Neighbourhood, 3 km, night | 10.1 / 646 / 4.68 | 9.8 / 508 / 2.90 |
| District, 3.8 km, day | n/a | 6.5 / 441 / 3.64 |
| Landmarks (7 views) | n/a | ≤ 10.0 / 345–589 / 3.5–5.2 |

Both runs sit at a capped 150–160 fps (6–6.5 ms median) in the headless session, so fps alone shows only that every view is far above the 30 fps target. The p95 frame time is at most 10 ms in every view, before and after. JS heap is 204–595 MB after, against 197–380 MB before. The medium and low tiers were also captured (VISUAL_QA.md). Low roughly halves the triangles at street level.

Not measured: GPU memory directly, integrated GPUs, and phones. For those, the `medium` and `low` tiers apply automatically, or with `?quality=`.

## 6. How to regenerate

Requirements: Node 22, Python 3.12 with `numpy`, `shapely`, `Pillow` and `rasterio` (`pip install rasterio`), and the `overturemaps` CLI. No credentials are needed for any step.

```bash
# 1. Overture (targeted bbox download, about 550 MB)
B=80.7501301,26.6500106,81.149975,27.0499301
for t in building building_part land_use land_cover water; do overturemaps download --bbox $B -f geojsonseq -t $t -o data/raw/overture_$t.geojsonseq; done
# 2. Google Open Buildings 2.5D heights (about 300 MB read over HTTP ranges, 5-10 min), then per-building stats
npm run data:heights
# 3. Ground surface (WorldCover + DEM + land use)
npm run data:ground
# 4. Buildings -> suppression -> vegetation -> HLOD bake -> validation
npm run data:city
```

`npm run data:city` runs `buildings`, `suppress`, `vegetation`, `bake` and `validate:city`. The baker refuses tiles that were not rebuilt, and the app refuses outdated HLOD or tiles, so a half-regenerated dataset cannot show a mixed city.

## 7. Known limitations

- **Heights are a model.** Google 2.5D heights are model estimates. Against the 134 buildings that also have a source floor count, the raster median reads **2.3 m lower** (mean absolute difference 4.1 m), so the city is probably slightly short overall and towers more so (one 32 m building reads 5.5 m). 870 raster values were rejected as tower bleed. 39% of buildings are inferred. See `HEIGHT_QUALITY_REPORT.md`.
- **Terrain is flat.** It stays at Y = 0. Lucknow's relief is about 25 m over 40 km and GLO-30 is a surface model (buildings and canopy included). Displacing the ground would need every road, label, marker and the camera re-based, for a barely visible gain. The DEM is used for baked shading, and the Gomti banks for ground classes.
- **Typology is coarse.** It relies on few source attributes. The old-city and heritage zones are hand-drawn boxes.
- **District view.** At 2–4 km, buildings below the relief rule show roofs without walls.
- **Landmarks are procedural approximations.** Vidhan Sabha is the weakest: its real outline extruded with a central dome.
- **Water** is the existing overlay shader. The river is not carved.
- **No post-processing chain.** Bloom, SSAO and colour grading were not added. Night glow uses emissive light, light pools and the district glow, to keep integrated GPUs viable.
- **Deployment not updated (by instruction).** `scripts/build_vercel.mjs` proxies only `overture_tiles_full`, `overture` and `hlod`. Before a deploy, add `ground` and `landmarks` to its `DATA_DIRS` and proxy route, or the deployed map will have no ground surface.
- **Dev servers lock tile files.** A dev server serving `public/` can briefly lock tile files on Windows. The pipeline writers retry for up to about 40 s; stopping the dev servers makes regeneration faster.
- **Not tested** on integrated GPUs or phones.
